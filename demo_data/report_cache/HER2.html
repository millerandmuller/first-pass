**Section 3: Class Effects & Known Target Toxicities**

HER2-directed therapeutics exhibit two cardinal class-level toxicities: cardiac dysfunction and embryo-fetal toxicity. Multiple approved agents carry boxed warnings for left ventricular dysfunction, which can manifest as decreased left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF) and congestive heart failure [PRIMARY FINDING]. This cardiotoxicity pattern appears across monoclonal antibody products targeting HER2, with treatment guidelines requiring evaluation of cardiac function prior to and during therapy, and discontinuation for confirmed clinically significant decreases in left ventricular function [PRIMARY FINDING]. The mechanistic basis for this toxicity is supported by genetic evidence: conditional cardiomyocyte-restricted knockout of Erbb2 in mice results in dilated cardiomyopathy, directly linking ERBB2 signaling to cardiac muscle maintenance in the adult heart [R-12].

Embryo-fetal toxicity represents the second consistent class effect. Exposure to HER2-targeted agents can cause embryo-fetal death and birth defects [PRIMARY FINDING]. This reproductive toxicity is anticipated given that Erbb2-null mice die in utero around embryonic day 10.5 due to failure of cardiac trabeculation and defects in cranial ganglia and neural crest derivatives [R-12], establishing HER2 signaling as essential for embryonic cardiovascular and neural development.

Nonclinical toxicology data across the class show that carcinogenicity studies have generally not been conducted for these biologics [R-1, R-2, R-4, R-5, R-6, R-7, R-8, R-9, R-11]. Mutagenicity testing of trastuzumab products consistently demonstrated no evidence of mutagenic activity in Ames bacterial or human peripheral blood lymphocyte assays at concentrations up to 5000 mcg/mL, and no chromosomal damage in mouse bone marrow micronucleus assays at doses up to 118 mg/kg [R-5, R-7, R-8, R-9, R-11]. One antibody-drug conjugate containing the cytotoxic payload DM1 showed aneugenic or clastogenic activity in an in vivo rat micronucleus assay and demonstrated testicular degeneration with hemorrhage in rats and ovarian corpus luteum hemorrhage and necrosis in females at severely toxic doses [R-3]. Multiple HER2-targeted monoclonal antibodies and tyrosine kinase inhibitors have received FDA approval across a range of HER2-positive cancer indications. The small-molecule HER2 inhibitor zongertinib (Hernexeos) represents a distinct mechanistic class [R-10]. Ado-trastuzumab emtansine (Kadcyla), an antibody-drug conjugate, has also been approved for HER2-positive indications [R-3].

Pertuzumab carries a boxed warning for left ventricular dysfunction and embryo-fetal toxicity [R-12]. Nonclinical toxicology data indicate that HER2-targeted agents have not been tested for carcinogenic potential across the monoclonal antibody class [R-1, R-2, R-4, R-5, R-6, R-7, R-8, R-9, R-11].
