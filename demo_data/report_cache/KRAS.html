3. Class Effects & Known Target Toxicities

The direct KRAS G12C inhibitors adagrasib, daraxonrasib, and sotorasib share no reproductive organ toxicity signals in nonclinical studies. Adagrasib induced vacuolation in ovaries and uterus, atrophy with mucification of vaginal mucosa in female rats, and atrophy with epithelial vacuolation of prostate and seminal vesicles in male rats at clinically relevant exposures; these findings largely reversed after treatment cessation [R-1]. Sotorasib produced no adverse effects on female or male reproductive organs in general toxicology studies in dogs and rats [R-7]. Daraxonrasib similarly showed no remarkable findings in reproductive organs of either sex in mice and monkeys [R-2]. This inconsistency across compounds with identical on-target mechanism argues against a class reproductive toxicity pattern.

Regorafenib, an anti-angiogenic multi-kinase inhibitor, demonstrated male reproductive findings including testicular tubular atrophy and degeneration, seminal vesicle atrophy, and epididymal oligospermia in rats at clinical exposures, alongside necrotic corpora lutea in female rat ovaries; similar findings occurred in dogs of both sexes at approximately 83% of human recommended-dose exposure [R-5]. Ramucirumab, a VEGFR2 antagonist, altered hormone levels critical for pregnancy and increased follicular cycle duration in monkeys, with dose-dependent follicular mineralization of the ovary in a 39-week study [R-6]. Panitumumab, an EGFR antibody, caused prolonged menstrual cycles and amenorrhea in female cynomolgus monkeys at 1.25 to 5 times the human dose, with decreased and delayed peak progesterone and estradiol; cycling resumed after treatment discontinuation [R-8]. These reproductive effects across regorafenib, ramucirumab, and panitumumab reflect their respective anti-angiogenic and EGFR-pathway mechanisms, not KRAS biology.

No germline KRAS loss-of-function precedent exists in adult humans; Kras-null mice die in utero around embryonic day 12.5-14 with defects in fetal liver erythropoiesis, confirming developmental lethality of complete loss-of-function [R-11]. This precludes inference of adult safety phenotypes from knockout models. The KRAS-targeted drug portfolio includes narrow G12C-specific inhibitors (adagrasib, sotorasib, daraxonrasib) and indirect modulators via parallel signaling nodes (MEK, VEGF, EGFR); no approved therapy carries a pan-KRAS mechanism-of-action label, limiting interpretability of a unified class toxicity profile.
